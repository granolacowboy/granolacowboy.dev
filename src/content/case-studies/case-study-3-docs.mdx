## Context

A boutique corporate law firm specialized in high-volume mergers and acquisitions. For every transaction, associates spent countless hours manually drafting repetitive ancillary documents (such as board consents, officer certificates, and disclosure schedules) by copying and pasting from previous deals. This process was inefficient and prone to human error, particularly when dealing with complex formatting and defined terms.

## Complication

The firm had previously attempted to use standard mail-merge tools, but these proved inadequate for the complex conditional logic required in legal documents. They needed a system that could dynamically include or exclude entire clauses based on specific deal parameters, adjust formatting automatically, and maintain perfect numbering. Additionally, the senior partners were skeptical of any technology that might compromise the bespoke feel of their work product.

## What I built

The document-assembly pipeline I built targeted the firm's M&A ancillary work. I started by auditing their existing document templates and standardizing the variable definitions. I then built a set of conditional templates on a document automation platform.

I built an interactive questionnaire for the associates. By answering a series of targeted questions about the deal structure, the system would automatically generate a complete package of customized first drafts. I also wrote custom Python scripts to integrate the assembly platform with the firm's document management system, ensuring that generated files were properly named and filed.
